The zeta function

Pick a number s. Raise every whole number to the power s, take the reciprocals, and add them all:

ζ(s) = 1/1s + 1/2s + 1/3s + 1/4s + …

This is the zeta function. ζ is the Greek letter zeta.

For s = 2 the sum is 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + 1/16 + … The terms shrink quickly enough that the total approaches a fixed number, 1.644934… In 1734 Euler showed that this number is exactly π²/6, where π is the usual circle constant.

For s = 1 the sum is 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4 + … The terms still shrink, but too slowly: the total grows without limit. The sum has a finite value exactly when s > 1.

So far s is an ordinary real number. In step 5 it becomes a complex number.

Key point: ζ(s) is defined by adding 1/ns over all whole numbers n. This sum works for s > 1.
